## 1. Speaking Skills

### Useful communication functions

| Function | Polite expression |
|---|---|
| Making a statement | I would like to explain my point. |
| Making a request | Could you please help me with this task? |
| Making an inquiry | Could you tell me when the class begins? |
| Disagreeing politely | I understand your point, but I see it differently. |
| Complaining | I would like to report a problem with the service. |
| Apologising | I am sorry for the inconvenience. |
| Asking for clarification | Could you please explain that again? |
| Giving an opinion | In my opinion, regular practice is important. |

### Short speaking framework

1. **Opening:** Good morning. Today I would like to talk about...
2. **Main point:** The main reason is...
3. **Support:** For example...
4. **Closing:** Thank you for listening. I would be happy to answer questions.

### Practice dialogue: Asking for information

**Student:** Excuse me, could you tell me where the department office is?
**Staff member:** It is on the second floor, beside the library.
**Student:** Thank you. Could you also tell me the office hours?
**Staff member:** It is open from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on working days.
**Student:** I appreciate your help.

### Oral-presentation checklist

- State the topic and purpose at the beginning.
- Use short, connected sentences.
- Speak at a steady pace and pronounce key terms clearly.
- Use signposting: first, next, finally.
- Do not read every word from a slide.
- End with a brief conclusion.

## 2. Introduction to Phonetics

### Three branches

- **Articulatory phonetics:** How speech sounds are produced by the speech organs.
- **Acoustic phonetics:** The physical properties of speech sounds as sound waves.
- **Auditory phonetics:** How speech sounds are received and interpreted by the listener.

### Basic sound categories

- **Vowels:** Sounds produced without a significant obstruction of airflow, such as /iː/ in *see* and /æ/ in *cat*.
- **Consonants:** Sounds produced with some obstruction or narrowing of airflow, such as /p/ in *pen* and /s/ in *see*.
- **Voiced sounds:** Vocal folds vibrate, e.g. /b/, /z/.
- **Voiceless sounds:** Vocal folds do not vibrate, e.g. /p/, /s/.

### Word stress

Word stress means giving one syllable greater prominence. Compare:

- **PREsent** (noun) and pre**SENT** (verb) in common pronunciation.
- **REcord** (noun) and re**CORD** (verb).

### Practice

Say each pair aloud and notice the sound difference:

- ship / sheep
- live / leave
- full / fool
- fan / van

**Exam-ready definition:** Phonetics is the scientific study of speech sounds, including their production, transmission and perception.

## 3. Listening Skills and Note-Taking

### Before, during and after listening

1. **Before:** Read the task and predict the kind of information required.
2. **First listening:** Identify the topic, speaker and purpose.
3. **Second listening:** Note names, dates, numbers, reasons and examples.
4. **After:** Check whether each answer is supported by what you heard.

### Effective note-taking

Use short phrases and symbols instead of copying complete sentences.

| Full expression | Quick note |
|---|---|
| because | b/c |
| for example | e.g. |
| increase | ↑ |
| decrease | ↓ |
| important | imp. |
| information | info. |

Do not invent abbreviations that you will not understand later. Leave space to add missing details.

### Listening practice script

Ask a friend to read this paragraph twice, or use a text-to-speech reader:

> The science club will organise a workshop on Saturday at 10 a.m. in Room 204. Participants should register by Thursday. The workshop will introduce basic laboratory safety and demonstrate how to record observations. Students should bring a notebook and a pen.

**Questions**

1. When will the workshop take place?
2. Where will it be held?
3. What is the registration deadline?
4. Name two items participants should bring.

**Answer key:** 1. Saturday at 10 a.m. 2. Room 204. 3. Thursday. 4. A notebook and a pen.

## 4. Reading Literary and General Essays

### Reading strategies

- **Skimming:** Read quickly to understand the overall topic.
- **Scanning:** Search for a specific name, date, number or keyword.
- **Close reading:** Examine wording, evidence, tone and logical connections.
- **Inference:** Draw a reasonable conclusion supported by the text.
- **Contextual vocabulary:** Infer a word's meaning from nearby words and the whole sentence.

### Literary communication: what to notice

1. Theme — the central idea explored by the text.
2. Tone — the writer's attitude.
3. Imagery — language that creates sensory pictures.
4. Metaphor — an implied comparison.
5. Evidence — words or details that support an interpretation.

**Exam-ready distinction:** Skimming is rapid reading for the general idea, whereas scanning is rapid reading to locate specific information.

## 5. Précis Writing

### Method

1. Read the passage carefully at least twice.
2. Identify the central argument and essential supporting points.
3. Remove examples, repetition, decorative details and minor facts.
4. Write in your own words without changing the author's meaning.
5. Give a suitable title if required.
6. Check unity, grammar, accuracy and the requested length.

### Original practice passage

Public libraries remain valuable even when digital information is widely available. They provide access to books, newspapers, computers and reliable learning resources. They also offer a quiet place for study and support people who may not have suitable internet access at home. Librarians help readers locate information and develop research skills. Therefore, libraries should adapt to changing technology rather than be considered unnecessary.

### Model précis

Public libraries continue to support education by providing learning resources, study spaces, digital access and research assistance. They should adapt to technological change to remain useful.

**Why it works:** It preserves the central claim and key reasons while removing repeated or less important details.

## 6. Analytical Writing

### A practical structure

1. **Introduction:** Introduce the issue and state your main argument.
2. **Analysis:** Explain causes, patterns or important factors.
3. **Evidence:** Use relevant facts, examples or credible sources.
4. **Evaluation:** Discuss limitations, alternatives or consequences.
5. **Conclusion:** Answer the central question based on the analysis.

### Example topic: Should students use AI tools for learning?

**Weak claim:** AI is good for students.

**Stronger analytical claim:** AI tools can support students by explaining difficult concepts and providing practice, but their educational value depends on verification, independent problem-solving and responsible use.

The stronger claim is more useful because it identifies benefits, conditions and limitations.

### Useful analytical language

- Cause: because, due to, as a result of
- Evidence: the data suggests, for example, according to
- Contrast: however, whereas, in contrast
- Evaluation: this indicates, this may suggest, a limitation is
- Conclusion: therefore, overall, in conclusion

## 7. Scientific Writing

### Common features

- Clear and precise language
- Logical organisation
- Evidence-based statements
- Correct units, symbols and terminology
- Reproducible methods where applicable
- Proper acknowledgement and citation of sources

### Basic laboratory-report structure

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Title | Experiment name |
| Objective | What the experiment aims to determine |
| Theory | Relevant principle and equations |
| Apparatus | Equipment and materials |
| Procedure | Steps followed |
| Observation | Recorded measurements |
| Calculation | Formula, substitution and units |
| Result | Final measured or calculated value |
| Discussion | Errors, limitations and interpretation |
| Conclusion | What the experiment established |

### Example: reporting a measurement

**Less precise:** The current was very high.

**More precise:** The measured current was 0.25 A under the stated circuit conditions.

Never change or invent experimental readings to make a result look better.

## 8. Commercial Correspondence

### Common types

- Enquiry letter — asking for product, price or service information.
- Quotation letter — providing price and terms.
- Order letter — placing an order.
- Acknowledgement — confirming receipt.
- Adjustment letter — responding to a complaint.
- Application or request letter — seeking information or action.

### Sample: Enquiry about a quotation

**Subject: Enquiry About Laboratory Equipment**

Dear Sir/Madam,

Our department is planning to purchase basic laboratory equipment for student practical sessions. We would appreciate it if you could send us your current product catalogue, price list, warranty terms and estimated delivery time.

Please also mention any educational discount available for institutional purchases.

Thank you for your assistance. We look forward to your response.

Yours faithfully,
Department Representative

### Checklist

- Is the purpose clear in the subject?
- Are product names, quantities and dates accurate?
- Is the requested action explicit?
- Is the tone polite and concise?
- Are attachments or references mentioned where necessary?

## 9. Tender Notice and Pre-Qualification Notice

### Typical information in a tender notice

- Name of the procuring organisation
- Tender or package identification number
- Description and quantity of goods or scope of work
- Eligibility requirements
- Tender document collection or submission instructions
- Submission deadline, date and time
- Opening details, where applicable
- Contact details and official instructions

### Original practice template — Tender Notice

**ABC Institute**
**Invitation for Tender (IFT)**

Tender Reference: ABC/PROC/2026/01
ABC Institute invites eligible suppliers to submit tenders for the supply of laboratory equipment.

Interested bidders must collect the tender document and submit their proposals according to the instructions stated in that document. The submission deadline is 12:00 noon on 15 December 2026.

Further information may be obtained from the Procurement Office during official working hours.

**Authorised Officer**
ABC Institute

*This is a fictional classroom template. It is not a real procurement notice.*

### Pre-qualification notice: essential contents

1. Project or procurement description.
2. Minimum relevant experience.
3. Required technical qualifications.
4. Financial-capacity requirements.
5. Documents to be submitted.
6. Submission method and deadline.
7. Evaluation and communication procedure.

**Exam distinction:** A tender notice invites bids for a specified procurement, while a pre-qualification notice first identifies applicants who meet stated eligibility criteria.

## 10. Report Writing

### Basic report structure

1. Title
2. Date and prepared by
3. Purpose or introduction
4. Method or activities
5. Findings
6. Discussion
7. Recommendations (if requested)
8. Conclusion

### Original sample: Report on a Student Workshop

**Report on a Student Workshop on Cybersecurity Awareness**
**Date:** 10 November 2026
**Prepared by:** Student Representative

A workshop on cybersecurity awareness was organised for first-semester students on 10 November 2026. The session introduced common online risks, including phishing, weak passwords and unsafe downloads.

The instructor demonstrated how to identify suspicious links, create strong passwords and enable multi-factor authentication. Students also discussed safe ways to protect personal information.

The workshop helped participants understand basic digital safety practices. It is recommended that similar sessions be arranged regularly.

### Report-writing reminders

- Separate observation from personal opinion.
- Use past tense for completed events.
- Include relevant dates, numbers and sources.
- Do not invent survey findings or quotations.
- Keep recommendations connected to the findings.

## 11. Integrated Practice

Try these tasks without looking at the sample answers above.

1. **Speaking:** Give a one-minute introduction to your CSE programme.
2. **Phonetics:** Explain the difference between a vowel and a consonant sound, with two examples.
3. **Listening:** Ask a partner to read the listening script and answer all four questions.
4. **Reading:** Read a short newspaper editorial and identify its main claim and supporting evidence.
5. **Précis:** Write a concise précis of the practice passage in Section 5.
6. **Analytical writing:** Analyse one benefit and one limitation of online learning.
7. **Scientific writing:** Write the objective and conclusion of an Ohm's law experiment.
8. **Commercial correspondence:** Draft an enquiry letter asking a supplier for the price of laboratory components.
9. **Tender notice:** Identify the essential information in the sample notice.
10. **Report:** Write a short report on a seminar held at your college.
