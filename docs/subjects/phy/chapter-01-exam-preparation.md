# Physics Chapter 01 Exam Preparation: Electrostatics and Capacitance

## 1. Quick concept map

- **Charge:** matter's electrical property; like charges repel and unlike charges attract.
- **Electric field:** force per unit positive test charge.
- **Electric potential:** potential energy per unit charge.
- **Capacitance:** charge stored per unit potential difference.
- **Gauss's law:** relates net electric flux through a closed surface to enclosed charge.

## 2. Exam-ready definitions

**Electric charge:** Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical interaction. Its SI unit is coulomb (C).

**Coulomb's law:** The electrostatic force between two stationary point charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

**Electric field intensity:** Electric field intensity at a point is the force experienced per unit positive test charge placed at that point.

**Electric potential:** Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive test charge in bringing it from infinity to that point, without changing its kinetic energy.

**Electric flux:** Electric flux measures the amount of electric field passing through a surface.

**Capacitance:** Capacitance is the ratio of charge stored on a conductor to its potential.

| Capacitance | \(C=Q/V\) | F |

Here \(k=1/(4\pi\epsilon_0)\approx9.0\times10^9\,Nm^2/C^2\). In the flux formula, \(\theta\) is the angle between the electric field and the surface's **area vector** (normal), not the plane itself.

## 4. Important reasoning

### Why is the electric field inside a conductor zero in electrostatic equilibrium?

If an electric field existed inside a conductor, its free charges would continue to move. In electrostatic equilibrium, charges have redistributed until the internal electric field becomes zero. Excess charge resides on the conductor's surface.

### Why do electric field lines never intersect?

At any point, the electric field has one definite direction. If two field lines intersected, the field would have two directions at the same point, which is impossible.

### Why is the electric field perpendicular to an equipotential surface?

Moving a charge along an equipotential surface requires no work. Therefore, the electric field has no tangential component along that surface; it is normal to it.

### What does a dielectric do?

A dielectric becomes polarized in an electric field. For a parallel-plate capacitor with the dielectric fully inserted, capacitance increases by the dielectric constant \(\kappa\), provided geometry is unchanged.

## 5. Solved numerical examples

### Example 1: Coulomb's law

Two charges \(2\mu C\) and \(3\mu C\) are separated by 0.30 m in vacuum. Find the force magnitude.

\[
F=k\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}
=9\times10^9\frac{(2\times10^{-6})(3\times10^{-6})}{(0.30)^2}
=0.60\,N
\]

Both charges are positive, so the force is repulsive.

### Example 2: Electric field

Find the field 0.20 m from a \(4\mu C\) point charge.

\[
E=k\frac{|q|}{r^2}
=9\times10^9\frac{4\times10^{-6}}{0.20^2}
=9.0\times10^5\,N/C
\]

### Example 3: Capacitor charge and energy

A \(6\mu F\) capacitor is connected to 10 V.

\[
Q=CV=6\times10^{-6}\times10=60\,\mu C
\]
\[
U=\frac12CV^2=\frac12(6\times10^{-6})(100)
=300\,\mu J
\]

### Example 4: Parallel-plate capacitor with dielectric

A capacitor has air capacitance 2 nF. A dielectric of relative permittivity 4 completely fills the gap. Find the new capacitance.

\[
C'=\kappa C=4\times2=8\,nF
\]

## 6. Short questions and answers

1. **What is the SI unit of charge?** Coulomb (C).
2. **What is the value of elementary charge?** Magnitude \(1.602\times10^{-19}C\).
3. **State the principle of superposition.** The net force or field due to several charges is the vector sum of the individual contributions.
4. **What is an electric dipole?** A pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
5. **Define one farad.** A capacitor has capacitance 1 F if 1 C of charge produces a potential difference of 1 V.
6. **Can two equipotential surfaces intersect?** No; that would assign two potentials to the same point.
7. **What is the net electric flux through a closed surface containing no net charge?** Zero, by Gauss's law.
8. **Where does excess charge reside on a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium?** On its surface.

## 7. Descriptive questions: answer outlines

### State and explain Gauss's law

Gauss's law states that the total electric flux through any closed surface equals the net charge enclosed divided by the permittivity of free space.

\[
\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec A=\frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_0}
\]

Explain the meaning of a closed Gaussian surface, enclosed charge, and area vector. Mention that the law is always valid, but it is most useful for highly symmetric charge distributions.

### Derive the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor

For plate area \(A\), separation \(d\), and charges \(+Q\) and \(-Q\), neglecting edge effects:

\[
E=\frac{Q}{\epsilon_0 A},\qquad V=Ed=\frac{Qd}{\epsilon_0 A}
\]
\[
C=\frac{Q}{V}=\frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}
\]

With a dielectric fully filling the gap, replace \(\epsilon_0\) by \(\kappa\epsilon_0\).

### Explain energy stored in a capacitor

Work is required to move charge onto capacitor plates. The stored energy is:

\[
U=\frac12QV=\frac12CV^2=\frac{Q^2}{2C}
\]

## 8. Common mistakes

- Use charge in coulombs and distance in metres before substitution.
- Coulomb force and electric field are vectors; direction matters.
- Potential is scalar; electric field is vector.
- In \(EA\cos\theta\), angle is measured from the area vector.
- Do not confuse \(C\) (capacitance) with coulomb (unit of charge); use context and units.
- The parallel-plate formula assumes a plate separation much smaller than plate dimensions and neglects fringing.
