**Course Code:** 510207
**Course Title:** Physics
**Credits:** 3.0
**Marks:** 80
**Class Hours:** 45
**Semester:** CSE First Semester

## Course Overview

The Session 2017–2018 NU CSE syllabus reference lists topics on electric current and electrical measurements, magnetic fields and force on current, and magnetic properties of matter. These notes explain the concepts from beginner level and add clearly labelled supporting material where useful.

> **Important scope distinction:** Electrostatics and capacitance are included as supplementary foundation notes in this repository. They do not appear in the Physics (510207) topic outline on pages 5–6 of the cited 2017–2018 syllabus copy. Study them if your teacher includes them or if they help you understand later topics, but do not assume they are examinable in your session without confirmation.

## Syllabus Topics in the 2017–2018 Reference

### 1. Electric Current, Simple Circuits and Electrical Measurements

- Electric current and Ohm's law
- EMF and potential difference
- Wheatstone bridge
- Simple RC and RL circuits
- Potentiometer
- Moving-coil galvanometer
- Ammeter and voltmeter
- Multimeter and wattmeter

### 2. Magnetic Field and Force on Current

- Magnetic field and field strength
- Magnetic force on current
- Directions of current and field
- Maxwell's screw rule
- Fleming's left-hand and right-hand rules
- Magnetic field near a long wire
- Magnetic field inside a solenoid

### 3. Magnetic Properties of Matter

- Magnetic poles and dipoles
- Coulomb's law for magnets and Gauss's theorem of magnetism
- Diamagnetism, paramagnetism and ferromagnetism
- Magnetomotive force and field intensity
- Self and mutual inductance
- Coefficient of magnetic coupling
- Rise and decay of current in inductive circuits
- Energy in a magnetic field
- Inductors in series and parallel
- Hysteresis and eddy-current losses

## Study Approach

For each topic:

1. Learn the standard definition, law or principle.
2. Write the formula with every variable and SI unit.
3. Follow the derivation or physical reasoning.
4. Solve numerical examples step by step.
5. Attempt the practice questions without looking at the answer.

Practice questions on this site are not labelled as NU previous-year questions unless the original paper and year have been verified.
