## 1. Objective
To study and experimentally verify experiment 05: verification of superposition theorem using instructor-approved apparatus and measured observations.

In a linear circuit with multiple independent sources, the voltage or current in any element equals the algebraic sum of the contributions produced by each independent source acting alone. When considering one source, deactivate the other independent ideal voltage sources by shorting them and ideal current sources by opening them. Dependent sources remain active.

## 5. Procedure
Construct the assigned linear resistive circuit with at least two independent sources. Measure the selected branch current/voltage with all sources active. Then consider one independent source at a time, deactivating the others correctly, and record the signed contribution. Restore the original circuit between tests. Add contributions algebraically and compare with the all-source measurement.

## 7. Calculations
Result from superposition = contribution 1 + contribution 2 (+ further contributions). Percentage difference = |measured − sum|/|measured| × 100 when measured is non-zero.

## 8. Result and discussion
The algebraic sum should be close to the all-source measurement. Superposition applies to linear voltage/current responses, not directly to power because power is nonlinear in voltage/current.

## 9. Precautions
- Switch off the supply before wiring or changing connections.
- Check polarity, meter mode, range and component ratings before switching on.
- Use current limiting and begin with the lowest practical source setting.
- Never connect an ammeter in parallel with a source; never short an unknown source.
- Record actual readings with units; do not fabricate observation values.
- Follow the instructor's bench-safety directions and the component datasheets.
