## 1. Objective
To study and experimentally verify experiment 06: study of an rc circuit and time constant using instructor-approved apparatus and measured observations.

An RC circuit contains a resistor R and capacitor C. Its time constant is τ = RC seconds. During charging from a DC step, capacitor voltage is vc(t) = Vs(1 − e^(−t/RC)); during discharging from V0, vc(t) = V0e^(−t/RC). After one time constant, charging reaches about 63.2% of its final value and discharging falls to about 36.8% of its initial value.

## 7. Calculations
Theoretical τ = RC. Experimental τ can be estimated as the charging time to 0.632Vs or discharging time to 0.368V0. Compare theoretical and experimental values.

## 8. Result and discussion
The voltage changes exponentially, not linearly. Timing resolution, capacitor tolerance, leakage and meter input resistance affect the result.

## 10. Viva voce
1. Define time constant. 2. Give the unit of τ. 3. What percentage is reached after one τ during charging? 4. What remains after one τ during discharging? 5. Why use a resistor to discharge a capacitor?

### Short answers
τ = RC and is measured in seconds. A resistor limits discharge current and reduces the risk of a damaging spark or component stress.
